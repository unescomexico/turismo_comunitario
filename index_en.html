Journeys with identity, local hosts and living heritage

The catalogue brings together experiences that can be booked or discussed directly with their hosts. Explore the territory, compare activities and discover nature, culture, gastronomy and community knowledge to plan your next visit.

01

History and archaeology

Walk through landscapes, histories and communities that keep their territory alive.

01

History and archaeology

Journeys connecting past and present

Visit archaeological sites, Maya communities and places of memory alongside people who know the territory. Each experience offers a living, community-based perspective on local history.

02

Nature

Adventures guided by local knowledge

Discover hiking, kayaking, wildlife watching, swimming and tours through nature reserves. Visits support community economies connected with caring for landscapes and biodiversity.

03

Crafts and knowledge

Learn directly from the people preserving these crafts

Take part in workshops on embroidery, pottery, fibres, leather and other craft processes. Along with a handmade piece, you will discover the stories, materials and knowledge that make every creation unique.

04

Gastronomy

Flavours that tell the story of a place

Discover traditional cuisines, cacao, honey, bread and milpa products through tastings and workshops. Check availability and contact hosts from each experience page.

Community-based and sustainable experiences

The experiences are presented using criteria that help identify authentic initiatives connected with heritage and delivering benefits for host communities.

01

Community management

The groups welcoming visitors are the first to benefit. Decisions are made collectively, and the wider community benefits both directly and indirectly.

02

Heritage

Each experience involves and safeguards at least one form of heritage: tangible heritage, such as archaeological sites and architecture; intangible heritage, such as knowledge, languages and traditions; or natural heritage, such as ecosystems and biodiversity.

03

Authenticity

Tourism services genuinely reflect the community’s cultural identity. They are not staged representations, but real practices rooted in the territory and the lives of its residents.

04

Local economic benefits

Tourism income is distributed among community members. The products and services used are locally sourced, reducing dependence on outside intermediaries.

05

Gender equality

Activities encourage equal participation by people of all genders as guides, hosts and decision-makers. Many experiences place particular emphasis on women’s leadership.

06

Decentralisation

Activities are organised and operated directly by community members, preventing benefits from being captured exclusively by intermediaries from outside the territory.

07

Inclusion and accessibility

The experiences aim to be accessible to as many people as possible, taking account of the natural characteristics of each setting and seeking adaptations where feasible.

08

Sustainable impact

Tourism provides an alternative source of income that supports the revival of traditional activities and the responsible use of natural resources without compromising their future conservation.
